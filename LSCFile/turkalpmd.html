We keep the physiology that bedside monitors discard, and build clinical AI that holds up to scrutiny.

The Digital Health and Artificial Intelligence in Critical Care Laboratory works inside the pediatric intensive care units of Hacettepe University in Ankara. It is directed by Izzet Turkalp Akbasli, MD, within the Life Support Center founded by Prof. Benan Bayrakci.

A data science lab next to the beds it studies

Founded in 2021 within the Hacettepe University Life Support Practice and Research Center, the lab joins second-by-second physiologic data with the engineering needed to keep it, govern it and learn from it.

Our clinicians are also the people who write the pipelines. We develop machine learning for continuous physiologic telemetry, for acute kidney injury and low cardiac output syndrome after congenital heart surgery, and for extracorporeal therapies such as extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) and continuous kidney replacement therapy (CKRT).

A second line asks where clinical AI fails: models that lose accuracy when they move between hospitals, language models working in Turkish records, and vision-language models that can be steered by text inside an image. Our multicenter kidney injury study, for example, reports how widely external performance varied between centers, because that variation decides whether a model is safe to deploy.

The lab is one of the Life Support Center's laboratories, alongside xenotransplantation, hybrid membranes and extracorporeal circuit research, and works with the same clinical teams and the same weekly research board.

NAFIS

Critical Patient Integrated Data and Research Platform

Every monitored child in our units produces physiologic data at second-level resolution. The hospital network already carries these streams, waveform packets included. The hospital information system keeps only minute-level values, and waveforms are deleted from monitor memory shortly after they are drawn. The data is produced and transported, but not recorded. NAFIS records it.

NAFIS listens to the existing device stream without writing anything back, joins it with laboratory, medication, procedure and note data already held by the hospital, and keeps the result inside the hospital network under parental consent and Hacettepe University's institutional data governance policy. It needs no new monitor integration and no additional integration cost. To our knowledge, no pediatric critical care data bank of this kind exists in Türkiye; the comparable resources are WAVES at Stanford, the Texas Children's real-time platform, CHU Sainte-Justine, PIC, MIMIC-IV, HiRID and AmsterdamUMCdb.

NAFIS is a data center, not a laboratory. Our lab designs and runs its architecture and supplies the analytic methods for the studies that use it.

  1. 1Sources

    • Bedside monitors: heart rate, invasive and non-invasive pressures, SpO₂, respiratory rate, etCO₂, temperature, central venous pressure; ECG, plethysmography and pressure waveforms
    • Mechanical ventilators: mode, settings, measured pressures, volumes and flows, alarms
    • Hospital information system: laboratory and microbiology, medications and blood products, procedures, nursing records, severity scores, reports and notes
    • ECMO, CKRT and plasma exchange consoles: structured bedside entry by the physician on duty

  2. 2Ingestion

    A read-only Kafka consumer gathers short segments, writes each one to disk, records it in the catalog, and only then advances its offset.

    The broker keeps 72 hours of messages, so a service outage or hardware failure is replayed without loss. Hospital records arrive through incremental, read-only SQL with a dedicated service account.

  3. 3Storage

    Waveform archive
    Compressed Apache Parquet partitioned by device, data type and date. Device ID, timestamp and values only, never a name or record number.
    Catalog and clinical tables
    PostgreSQL, with constraints that block overlapping time intervals for a device.
    Identity vault
    A separate, restricted schema that links consented children to their random IDs.

  4. 4Research access

    An approved study receives only the variables and time windows it asked for, keyed by random IDs such as NAFIS-004217.

    Analyses run on the in-network server. Only aggregate results leave, after the principal investigator approves them.

Safeguards

  • Read-only access to every source. Devices and hospital systems are never written to or reconfigured.
  • Intranet only. The portal accepts connections from hospital IP ranges, and outbound traffic is closed at the firewall.
  • Disks and backups are encrypted. Master keys are split with Shamir's secret sharing into five shares, any three of which rebuild them: no single person can decrypt, and losing two shares loses nothing.
  • Encrypted incremental backups twice a day to two NAS units at separate sites, immutable snapshots, and a restore test every three months.
  • Personal accounts, role-based permissions enforced on the server, and an immutable audit log of every access, query and export.

Consent

  1. From admission, device data are stored against bed and time only, linked to no child and visible to no researcher.
  2. When the child is stable, a pediatric critical care fellow informs the family and asks for consent, aiming for the first 72 hours. Children aged 7 and older with decision capacity give their own assent on age-appropriate forms.
  3. With consent, the child receives a random ID and the stay moves into the research layer under that ID.
  4. Without consent, the pre-record is deleted unopened by monthly board decision. Bereaved families are not approached; those deletions are counted to monitor selection bias.

The model travels to the data. The data stays.

Patient-level data never leave Hacettepe. When an external group wants to work with NAFIS, its model is trained or validated inside the hospital network after research board and ethics approval. Only model weights and aggregate performance metrics leave, and the weights are first tested for memorization and membership inference.

How Claude will work in this lab

We are a small group of clinician-scientists building infrastructure that would normally need a software team. Claude extends that team. The boundary is fixed by our own protocol: researchers do not upload NAFIS data to external AI services, cloud language models included.

Hacettepe hospital network

Stays here

  • Patient-level physiologic and clinical data
  • Identity vault and encryption key shares
  • Model training and validation on real records
  • Queries, approvals and the audit log

What crosses

Reviewed before it moves

  • Source code, tests and infrastructure definitions
  • Schemas, data dictionaries and mapping tables
  • Synthetic and simulated signals
  • Aggregate tables and figures approved by the principal investigator

Claude

Works on these

  • Claude Code writes, reviews and tests our software
  • Claude drafts protocols, analysis plans and manuscripts
  • Shared projects keep standards, style and decisions consistent across the team

Never: identifiable records, waveforms or notes from real patients.

NAFIS engineering
The read-only Kafka consumer, idempotent ingestion, nightly Parquet compaction, catalog constraints, backup and restore verification, and the tests around each; container builds and version-controlled documentation, so the system outlives any one person's tenure.
Standards and interoperability
HL7 v2 (ADT, ORU, ORM) and FHIR R4 resources mapped to the OMOP common data model; LOINC, SNOMED CT, ICD-10 and ATC mappings; national Turkish health data dictionaries; our OMOP extension for CKRT.
Analysis code that can be audited
Modeling pipelines for kidney injury, low cardiac output and deterioration, written and reviewed against TRIPOD+AI and PROBAST+AI, then executed inside the hospital network.
Governance documents
Ethics protocols, data request and data use forms, consent and assent forms in age-appropriate Turkish, and variable dictionaries for each approved study.
Writing and review
Manuscripts, grant applications to TÜBİTAK and Horizon Europe, systematic review appraisal, and structured internal peer review before submission.
Teaching
Pediatric critical care fellows learn data science on simulated NAFIS data before they touch a real record.

We already build on Claude. The lab publishes open-source Claude skills for scientific writing and for agentic data analysis, and uses Claude Code to maintain the Life Support Center website and its bedside calculators, this page included.
